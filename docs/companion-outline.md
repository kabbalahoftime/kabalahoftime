time to the two thousand, then back down to today. Ma'alot, then Mi'utim. The
form teaches the content before a word is read.

Five of the counts are given rather than chosen, marked *(structural)* below.
Only III's spacing, IV and VI are arbitrary, and those are the ones to move.

---

## I · The First Light — 8 days

The claim the whole book rests on: **creation is first of all the creation of
time.** "Let there be light," and immediately evening and morning, and *one
day* — a day counted before there was anyone to count it.

Eight: the seven days of creation, and the eighth — the day outside the week,
where creation is handed over to us to continue. *(structural)*

**Already written.** Reflections' first piece, Bereshit, treats Rashi's opening
question: why the Torah does not begin at the first mitzvah given to Israel,
*haChodesh hazeh lachem*, the sanctification of the new moon. The first
commandment to a people is a commandment about time. That is day 1 or 2.

---

## II · The Thirty-Two Paths — 32 days

*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1 names its own number in its first line: *בשלשים ושתים
נתיבות פליאות חכמה* — thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom. **Ten Sefirot and
twenty-two letters.** ל״ב is *lev*, the heart; the Torah's last letter and its
first spell it. *(structural)*

- 10 days — the Sefirot, one each
- 22 days — the letters, one each

**This is where letters become time.** Sefer Yetzirah divides its own
twenty-two into three mothers, seven doubles and twelve simples, and maps them
to the seasons, the days of the week and the months. The alphabet is already a
calendar. That is the hinge into Movement III.

The Four Worlds belong here as framing, not as a count — they are later
(Zohar, the Ari) and only borrow Yetzirah's name.

**Already written.** The Alef Bet book: 41 pages, two letters to each of the
sixteen cycles, with the creatures that sing for them and the mishnayot that
count for them.

---

## III · The Climb — 21 days

Seven rungs above the day, three days each: **week, month, season, year,
shmita, jubilee, millennium.**

Each rung: what it is, where the Torah counts it, what it holds.

The jubilee hands straight to the millennium. No centuries — nothing in this
tradition counts by hundreds. The millennium earns its place because a
thousand years is one of God's days (Psalms 90:4), which is the only reason
the next movement means anything.

---

## IV · The Two Thousand — 14 days

The Torah preceded the world by two thousand years — *"a delight **day by
day**"* (Proverbs 8:30), two divine days (Bereshit Rabbah 8:2).

Then history in four spans of two thousand: before the world, *tohu*, Torah,
the days of Mashiach — with the seventh millennium, the Shabbat, standing above
the four. Cited in Sanhedrin 97a from **Tanna d'vei Eliyahu**, which is the
app's own fifty-six.

Each two thousand is **forty jubilees** — and only the fiftieth makes it come
out even: forty forty-nines is 1,960, and the missing forty years are the
jubilee years themselves. The world's six thousand is 120 jubilees, the span
set on a man.

Closes on where we stand: year 5787, 1,787 years into the third span, 213 left.

---

## V · The Thirteen Months — 156 days

Twelve days to each of the thirteen months: the month itself, its letter, its
tribe, its sense, and the holidays inside it. 13 × 12 = 156, which is also
*Yosef*. *(structural)*

The descent begins here — from the millennium down to the year, and the year
read month by month.

**Already written.** Reflections: fifty-four pieces walking the same year by
its portions. The months and the portions run in step.

---

## VI · The Year's Own Cycles — 50 days

The five cycles the app counts across a year that are not the week:

| cycle | what it is |
|---|---|
| **91** | the season — four of them make 364, as four two-thousands make history |
| **56** | Tanna d'vei Eliyahu, over 728 half-days |
| **41** | nine cycles of forty-one |
| **28** | the Rambam whole, in fourteen |
| **13** | the attributes of mercy |

This is where the microcosm is shown plainly: **four spans of 2,000 in history,
four spans of 91 in the year, four faces in the day.** The same four at three
scales, and the app has been counting the middle one all along.

---

## VII · The Week — 52 days

One entry to each week of the year, carrying what the app already holds for it:
the Sefirah pairing, the creature from Perek Shirah with its verse, the mishnah
from Pirkei Avot, the trait.

Week 33 is Lag Ba'Omer — the donkey, Hod within Hod, the carrier.

**Already written.** The app's own week table — animal, rabbi, trait — for all
fifty-two. These entries are the table turned into prose.

---

## VIII · The Day — 31 days

Where the reader has been standing the whole time.

- 15 — the Ma'alot, Keter down to Malchut as the moon waxes
- 15 — the Mi'utim, the climb back as it wanes
- 1 — the thirty-first, the seal of a completed path, Yehoshua 10:12

*(structural)*

The last entry is the first entry at a different scale: the day that was
counted before anyone could count it, now counted by the reader.

## Seeds already in the author's voice

~3,700 words: 41 ⓘ notes at three depths, and the Help Guide. The concepts are
named in the right register; the flesh is what is missing.

Reflections (54 pieces) feeds V. The Alef Bet book feeds II. The app's week
table feeds VII.

And `tools/fetch_blog.py` brings down 677 posts, 623,452 words, from
kabbalahoftime.com — the source for anything summarised rather than composed.

## The rule

One entry a day, read the day it is written, in the context it describes. The
old companion's fabrications survived because nobody could read 47,000 words at
once. Written daily, each entry is read by the person who knows whether it is
true.
